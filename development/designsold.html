Build guide G.02 — design files · status: draft

The design files

Every part of OpenG1 starts from the Unitree G1's own meshes. The OpenSCAD files cut pockets for the actuators, add a frame where the printer needs one, and keep the outside shape. This guide shows how the files are organised, what was changed from the originals, every part you need, and how it all goes together.

§ 01

The robot in one file

— 01 / 08 —

Open it, pose it, export it

OpenScad/assembly/assembly.scad loads every part of the robot in the G1's torso frame and poses it with the G1's own joint angles. Change the numbers at the top of the file and press F5. Every printable part also has its own file, which you open, render with F6 and export as an STL.

qL, qR
arms, 7 angles each: shoulder pitch, roll, yaw, elbow, wrist roll, pitch, yaw
qLegL, qLegR
legs, 4 angles each: hip pitch, hip roll, hip yaw, knee
qWaist
waist yaw, turns the torso on the pelvis
torso_style
"framed" (aluminium frame + panels) or "solid" (old one-piece torso)
Angles
degrees, same signs as the G1 URDF on both sides
OpenSCAD
2024 or newer, with the Manifold backend for F6
§ 02

How the files are organised

The repository has two halves. OpenScad/ holds the files you open; STL/ holds the meshes they load. Each file you open sits exactly one folder below OpenScad/, and every mesh path is written as ../../STL/… from there. That is why the folders must stay as they are. If OpenSCAD can't find a mesh, it prints a warning in the console and shows an empty view.

Shared code lives in OpenScad/lib/. g1_servo_arm.scad has every arm and shoulder joint, torso_frame_lib.scad the frame and torso panels, and legs_lib.scad the pelvis, legs and waist. The part files and the assembly include them.

§ 03

Where the geometry comes from

— 03 / 08 —

No part is drawn by hand twice. Each subsystem has one Python script that holds every dimension, writes the OpenSCAD or STL files, and checks the result: actuators inside their links, walls at least 3 mm, joints swept through their G1 ranges, parts removable in their assembly order. Change the parameters and run the script again.

§ 04

What changed from the original G1 files

— 04 / 08 —

The rule throughout is to cut, not add. The outside of every link keeps the G1 shape. Material is only added where a wall around an actuator would be thinner than 3 mm, and it stays inside the original outline. Where a pocket opens to the outside, the piece that was removed comes back as a hatch that screws on.

§ 05

How it fits together

— 05 / 08 —

From the pelvis up

The pelvis is the base. It holds both hip-pitch actuators and the waist-yaw actuator, and each leg hangs from it joint by joint. The waist yaw link bolts onto the waist output and carries the torso. The torso is an aluminium frame: the shoulder blocks and top panel bolt onto its crossbars, the front and back panels close it, and the head bolts to the top panel. Each arm starts at the servo in its shoulder block.

Leg chain
pelvis → hip pitch link → hip roll link → thigh → shin
Waist
pelvis → waist yaw link → torso bottom plate
Torso
4 posts, 2 crossbars, 4 side rails, 10 printed parts
Arm chain
shoulder block → pitch → roll → yaw → elbow → wrist roll → wrist pitch → wrist yaw + hand
Head
4 × M3 up through the top panel into the collar

§ 07

Build order

— 07 / 08 —

  1. Export and print

    Render each file in parts_left/, parts_right/ and hardware/ with F6 and export it. The torso, head, pelvis, leg and waist parts are ready-made STLs. Print hatches and caps in the same material as their link so the seams disappear, and hubs in PETG, hex end down.

  2. Check your servos first

    Measure the shaft offset, horn diameter and horn height of the servos you bought and compare them with SERVO in generator/design.py. If they differ, change them and run emit.py before printing the arms.

  3. Build the pelvis

    Melt in the inserts, bolt each hip-pitch stator to its pelvis half, join the halves on the dowels, then fit the waist stator carrier and build the waist and hip-pitch actuators into their pockets.

  4. Build each leg downwards

    Hip pitch link, then the hip-roll actuator, roll link, thigh, knee actuator and shin. Each joint has to be finished before the next actuator goes in.

  5. Build the torso frame

    Screw the posts to the bottom plate, add the side rails and crossbars, then bolt the shoulder blocks (with their servos) and the top panel onto the crossbars.

  6. Join torso and pelvis

    Bolt the waist yaw link to the waist output through the bores in its riser, then lower the torso onto it and fit six M4 screws from inside the torso.

  7. Head, electronics, panels

    Screw the head to the top panel from inside, fit the trays with their boards, run the D455 cable down the neck hole, then fit the back and front panels last.

  8. Arms

    At each joint, drop the servo in, screw the hub to the horn, set the horn at the servo's centre, screw the hatch back on and slide the next link onto the hex.

Every actuator is a pinch hazard once powered. Test each joint on a bench supply before it goes into the robot, and keep fingers clear of the hips and knees.

§ 08

What has been checked

Each generator checks its own parts on the real geometry, so these hold for the files as published. None of it replaces a test print.

Arm servos
fully inside their links, every joint swept through its G1 range with no collisions
Torso frame
at least 5.5 mm inside the skin; no part overlaps; every panel slides off in one direction
Panel surface
94–95 % within 0.05 mm of the original G1 torso
Waist
clears the pelvis and actuator from −150° to +150°; at least 3 mm wall round the pocket
Right leg
matches the mirrored left to 0.0001 mm in a mirrored pose
