Build guide G.02 — design files · status: draft

The design files

Every part of OpenG1 starts from the Unitree G1's own meshes. Pockets are cut for the actuators and a frame is added where the printer needs one, while the outside shape stays the G1's. This guide shows how the files are organised, what was changed from the originals, every part you need, and how it all goes together.

§ 01

The robot in one file

— 01 / 08 —

Open it, pose it

STL/robot_assembly.scad loads every printed part of the robot and poses it with the G1's own joint chain, starting from the pelvis, with the feet on the floor at the zero pose. Open it in OpenSCAD, press F5 and move the joints with the sliders in the Customizer (Window → Customizer). STL/arms/arm_assembly.scad does the same for the two arms on their own.

§ 02

How the files are organised

Everything is in STL/. The assemblies load the meshes by relative path, so keep robot_assembly.scad in STL/ and arm_assembly.scad in STL/arms/. If OpenSCAD can't find a mesh, it prints a warning in the console and leaves that part out.

Each side's arm folder has the seven edited links in links/ and one folder per joint in actuators/ with that joint's cartridge, cap, horn plate and idler pin. The actuator parts are saved lying flat, ready to print. The links stay in their G1 frame, so orient them in the slicer.

Units
mm. Exception: the stock reference meshes in legs/feet/, torso/ and torso_waist_parts_unmodified/ are in metres, like the original G1 files

§ 03

Where the geometry comes from

— 03 / 08 —

The arm and hip-yaw parts are generated in Python, not drawn by hand. The script holds every dimension, cuts the stock G1 mesh, writes clean STLs and checks the result. Change the parameters and run it again. Each mesh is turned into a distance field, combined with the new features, rebuilt as a surface and simplified to within 0.02 mm. Every file comes out watertight, in one piece, at 10–80 k triangles.

The arm generator needs Python with numpy, scipy and scikit-image, and a C++ compiler for its small geometry library (g++ -O3 -shared -fPIC -o libgeom.so geom.cpp). The torso frame, pelvis, legs and waist were generated by earlier scripts that aren't in this repository. Their STLs are provided ready-made.

§ 04

What changed from the original G1 files

— 04 / 08 —

The rule throughout is to cut, not add. The outside of every link keeps the G1 shape. The only visible changes are screw holes, cable exits, and faces hidden under a neighbouring link that are trimmed 0.3–0.4 mm so joints don't rub. Material is only added where a G1 part was too thin to hold anything, and it stays inside the original outline.

§ 05

How it fits together

— 05 / 08 —

From the pelvis up

The pelvis is the base. It holds both hip-pitch actuators and the waist-yaw actuator, and each leg hangs from it joint by joint. The waist yaw link carries the torso, an aluminium frame with printed panels. The head bolts to the top panel. Each arm starts at the standard servo in its shoulder block, which drives the shoulder pitch link through a horn hub, as on the G1.

Leg chain
pelvis → hip pitch → hip roll → (hip yaw) thigh → shin → stock ankle and foot
Waist
pelvis → waist yaw link → torso bottom plate
Arm chain
shoulder block → pitch → roll → yaw → elbow → wrist roll → wrist pitch → wrist yaw
Arm actuator
a cartridge holding the servo, screwed into the link; the servo's plastic cross horn sits in a horn-shaped pocket

A standard servo's spline sits about 10 mm from one end, so it needs about a 64 mm circle round the joint axis. Only the shoulder roll and elbow positions have that much room inside the G1 outline. The other four arm joints use micro servos with their plastic mounting ears snipped off.

§ 07

Build order

— 07 / 08 —

  1. Print the fit tests

    Print STL/arms/fit_tests/ and one horn plate first. Check that the bearings press in, the pins fit, the inserts melt in cleanly and your servos and horns drop into their pockets. If anything is off, change BORE_PRESS, INS_D, CLR, SERVO or HORN in arms/generator/design.py and regenerate before printing the arms.

  2. Print everything else

    All parts are ready-made STLs in mm. The arm actuator parts are already lying flat; the links are in their G1 frame, so orient them in the slicer.

  3. Build the pelvis

    Melt in the inserts, bolt each hip-pitch stator to its pelvis half, join the halves on the dowels, then fit the waist stator carrier and build the waist and hip-pitch actuators into their pockets.

  4. Build each leg downwards

    Hip pitch link, then the hip-roll actuator and roll link. For hip yaw, bolt the actuator's output shaft to the top of the thigh first, build the rest of the actuator on it, then put it into the hip roll link's pocket and bolt it through the housing into the floor inserts. Then the knee actuator and shin.

  5. Build the torso frame

    Screw the posts to the bottom plate, add the side rails and crossbars, then bolt the shoulder blocks (with their servos) and the top panel onto the crossbars.

  6. Join torso and pelvis

    Bolt the waist yaw link to the waist output, then lower the torso onto it and fit six M4 screws from inside the torso.

  7. Head, electronics, panels

    Screw the head to the top panel from inside, fit the trays with their boards, run the D455 cable down the neck hole, then fit the back and front panels last.

  8. Arms

    For each joint: slide the cartridge into the link, drop in the servo, close it with its cap, screw the horn into its plate or pocket, set the servo at its centre and fit the centre screw. Then fit the next link and its screws, and the idler pin on yoke joints. The order for each joint is in STL/arms/README.md.

Every actuator is a pinch hazard once powered. Test each joint on a bench supply before it goes into the robot, and keep fingers clear of the hips and knees.

§ 08

What has been checked

These checks were run on the real geometry, so they hold for the files as published. None of it replaces a test print.

Meshes
every arm and hip-yaw STL is watertight, one piece, with consistent normals
Arm parts
no cartridge, cap, plate, pin or servo overlaps a link; links swept ±1 rad with no collisions beyond the stock G1's own
Arm walls
at least 0.7 mm round every pocket; at least 2.5 mm at the elbow
Right arm
built from Unitree's right-arm meshes, not a mirror of the left
Assemblies
arm part positions match the generator to within 0.0001 mm
Hip yaw
about 10–12 mm of wall round the pocket, at least 11 mm above each insert

Still to do

The .scad assemblies have been checked for paths and placement, but haven't been rendered in OpenSCAD itself yet. Please report any part that doesn't load.
